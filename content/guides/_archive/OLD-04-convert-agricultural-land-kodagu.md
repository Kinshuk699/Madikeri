To build a house on agricultural land in Kodagu you must first get the land diverted to non-agricultural use under section 95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964. Since [Karnataka Act 02 of 2023](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/Acts%20&%20Ordinance/02%20of%202023%20(E)%20Land%20revenue.pdf), you apply to the Deputy Commissioner with an affidavit, and the approval can come as fast as seven days, or be deemed granted if the office misses its deadline.

I'm selling [land in Galibeedu](https://landincoorg.in/), and I'm told this is the question that decides whether a Bangalore buyer proceeds, so I've quoted the Act directly rather than summarise blogs.

## The process, as the Act now describes it

The current wording of section 95(2) comes from [Karnataka Act 02 of 2023](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/Acts%20&%20Ordinance/02%20of%202023%20(E)%20Land%20revenue.pdf). The applicant submits an affidavit with the application to the Deputy Commissioner. What happens next depends on whether a Master Plan covers the land:

| Situation | Timeline the Act sets | If the office is late |
|---|---|---|
| The use matches a published Master Plan for that land | The DC issues the order within 7 days | Approval is deemed granted if no decision in 15 days |
| No published Master Plan, or the land is outside a local planning area | Other departments get 15 days to give their opinion; the DC then issues the order | If no opinion comes in 15 days, departments are deemed to have no objection. If the DC doesn't decide in 30 days, approval is deemed granted |

Two further conditions from the same section:

- If you want to divert only part of a survey number, you must file a pre-conversion sketch approved by the Survey Settlement and Land Records Department, marking the part to be converted.
- A conversion obtained with a false or wrong affidavit is null and void. The conversion fee, if paid, is forfeited, and the DC can order the land itself forfeited.

## What changed in 2025

[Karnataka Act 56 of 2025](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/uploads/media_to_upload1759555220.pdf) added two things worth knowing:

- Conversion is exempted for land parcels used for renewable energy projects, subject to the prescribed fee.
- Under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act's penalty table, selling agricultural land for a non-agricultural purpose without conversion carries a penalty of ₹1 lakh (with a carve-out for cases the government regularises under a scheme).

## Documents you will need

The Act names the affidavit and, for a partial conversion, the pre-conversion sketch. In practice the Deputy Commissioner's office also asks for records of the land. I have not found one official document list on the land conversion portal, so I am not inventing one. The records you should have ready, from the [due diligence checklist](/guides/due-diligence-checklist-coorg/), are the RTC, mutation register and khata, which you can pull from [Bhoomi](https://landrecords.karnataka.gov.in/).

## Timeline, summed up

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Apply with affidavit to the DC | Day 0 |
| Master Plan case: order issued | within 7 days |
| Master Plan case, no decision: deemed approval | after 15 days |
| No Master Plan: departments' opinion | within 15 days |
| No Master Plan, no decision: deemed approval | after 30 days |
| Partial conversion: pre-conversion sketch | filed with the application |

These are the deadlines the Act itself states. They start when the application is received.

## Cost: what I can and cannot quote

The prescribed conversion fee is levied under section 95(7), and the schedule sits in the Karnataka Land Revenue Rules, 1966, at Rule 107 and Rule 107-AA. The portal that publishes it is [landconversion.karnataka.gov.in](https://landconversion.karnataka.gov.in/).

- **I could not read the current rupee amounts.** The fee schedule the portal publishes is a scanned PDF with no text layer, and I have no way to read the numbers reliably. I am not quoting a fee I cannot verify.
- **A reference point, owner-stated:** the owner of the land we are selling has said he has budgeted roughly ₹13 lakh as his estimate for converting a parcel of this size in Kodagu. That is his estimate, not an official figure, and it covers the total exercise he has planned, not just the statutory fee.

For an exact quote, ask the Deputy Commissioner's office in Madikeri, or a Kodagu property lawyer, once you know the survey number and extent.

## FAQs

### How long does land conversion take in Karnataka?
Under Karnataka Act 02 of 2023, seven days if the use matches a published Master Plan (deemed approved after 15 days of silence). With no Master Plan, departments get 15 days to object and the DC must decide within 30 days, or approval is deemed granted.

### Who approves land conversion in Kodagu?
The Deputy Commissioner of Kodagu, under section 95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964.

### What does it cost to convert agricultural land in Kodagu?
I could not read the current fee schedule because the portal publishes it as a scanned PDF. The owner's own estimate for converting a parcel of this size is about ₹13 lakh. Ask the DC's office or a lawyer for the exact figure.

### Do I need a survey sketch for conversion?
Yes, if you are converting only part of a survey number. The Act requires a pre-conversion sketch approved by the Survey Settlement and Land Records Department.

### What happens if I sell land for building without converting it?
Since Karnataka Act 56 of 2025, selling agricultural land for a non-agricultural purpose without conversion carries a penalty of ₹1 lakh.
