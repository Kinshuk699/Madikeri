Yes. An Indian citizen living in India can buy agricultural land in Karnataka even if they are not a farmer and earn their income from a salary or a business, because [Karnataka Act 56 of 2020](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/Acts%20&%20Ordinance/56%20of%202020%20(E).pdf) removed the old income bar from the Karnataka Land Reforms Act. I could not find any later Act that brings it back.

I'm selling [land in Galibeedu](https://landincoorg.in/) myself, and Google gives conflicting answers to this question. So this page sticks to what the Acts themselves say, and links to each one.

## What the law used to say

From 1 March 1974, section 79A of the Karnataka Land Reforms Act, 1961 stopped a person or a family from acquiring agricultural land if they had an assured annual income from non-agricultural sources above a set limit. The limit was last raised to ₹25 lakh a year by Act 33 of 2015, and income was averaged over five consecutive years. Section 80, titled "Transfers to non-agriculturists barred", separately made it unlawful to sell land to "a person who is not an agriculturist".

For a salaried professional in Bangalore, those two sections were the reason buying farmland was difficult.

## What changed in 2020

| Date | Instrument (state-published) | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| 13 July 2020 | [Karnataka Ordinance 13 of 2020](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/karnataka/2020/Karnataka%2013%20of%202020.pdf) | Removed sections 79A, 79B and 79C. Narrowed section 80. Also raised the land ceiling in section 63 from 10 to 20 units. |
| 2 November 2020 | [Karnataka Ordinance 23 of 2020](https://www.indiaspend.com/uploads/2021/01/31/file_upload-399235.pdf) | Repeated the changes to sections 79A, 79B, 79C and 80. It does not include the section 63 change. |
| 30 December 2020 | [Karnataka Act 56 of 2020](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/Acts%20&%20Ordinance/56%20of%202020%20(E).pdf), [Gazette Extra-ordinary No. 719](https://kandaya.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/DPAL%2054%20SHASANA%202020.pdf) | Made the changes permanent. Sections 79A, 79B and 79C are treated as removed from 1 March 1974, and cases still pending under them were closed. |
| 10 September 2025 | [Karnataka Act 56 of 2025](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/uploads/media_to_upload1759555220.pdf) | Changed penalties and some land-conversion rules. It does not restore sections 79A or 79B. |

After Act 56 of 2020, section 80 is titled "Restrictions on transfer of certain lands" and no longer mentions agriculturists.

## What still restricts you

1. **A-class irrigated land.** Section 80 still bars selling A-class irrigated land to someone who will not use it for agriculture. Check the land classification on the RTC before you agree a price.
2. **Land granted to Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes.** New section 80-A says none of the relaxations apply to such granted land while the transfer ban under the Karnataka SC and ST (Prohibition of Transfer of Certain Lands) Act, 1978 is in force. Your lawyer should check whether the land was ever a government grant.
3. **Mortgages.** Section 81(2-A) allows agricultural land to be mortgaged only to the institutions the Act lists, such as banks and co-operative societies. A private lender cannot hold it as security.
4. **Building a house.** Buying agricultural land does not let you build a house on it. The land has to be converted (the law calls it "diversion") under section 95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964. Since Act 56 of 2025, selling agricultural land for a non-agricultural purpose without conversion carries a penalty of ₹1 lakh. The [conversion guide](/guides/convert-agricultural-land-kodagu/) covers the process.
5. **NRIs and OCIs.** This is a central rule, not a Karnataka one. The [Ministry of External Affairs summary](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf) of the foreign exchange rules says a non-resident Indian cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or a farm house in India. They can inherit it. The 2020 Karnataka change does not affect this. See [buying land in Coorg as an NRI](/guides/nri-buy-land-coorg/).
6. **Land ceiling.** Section 63 caps how much land one person or family can hold, measured in "units". One unit is one acre of A-class land, or an equivalent area of lower-class land under Schedule I of the Act. The July 2020 ordinance raised the cap from 10 to 20 units, but Act 56 of 2020, which replaced the ordinances, does not contain that change, and I could not find a state-published Act that does. So I cannot tell you for certain whether the cap is 10 units or 20. Either way it is far above what most weekend-home buyers purchase. If you are buying tens of acres, ask a lawyer.
7. **Coffee and other plantations.** Section 104 says the ceiling and section 80 do not apply to plantations, meaning land used mainly for plantation crops. This matters in Coorg, where much of the land is coffee. How a plantation is classified on the RTC is a question for your lawyer. See [coffee estate or dry land](/guides/coffee-estate-vs-dry-land-coorg/).

## Why Google gives conflicting answers

When I searched on 30 October 2026, Google's AI answer to "can a Bangalore software engineer buy agricultural land in Karnataka" said yes. Its answers to "how to buy land in Coorg" and "2 acre land for sale in Coorg to build a villa" said non-agriculturists face restrictions. The second answer reflects the law before 2020.

Part of the confusion is real. In August 2024 [The Hindu reported](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/govt-set-to-bring-back-provisions-in-land-reforms-act-that-bar-non-farmers-from-buying-land/article68547055.ece) that the state government planned to bring the restrictions back once it had a majority in the Legislative Council. That would need a new Act. I have not found one on the [state legislation department's website](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/en) as of 30 October 2026. If one is passed, I will update this page.

## What I could not verify

- Whether the section 63 ceiling is now 10 or 20 units (see point 6).
- Whether section 81A, which required certain declarations at the time of registration, still requires any declaration after the 2020 changes. Act 56 of 2020 does not amend it, and I could not find official guidance. Ask the Sub-Registrar's office or your lawyer.

## FAQs

### Is there still an income limit for buying farmland in Karnataka?
No. The ₹25 lakh non-agricultural income limit was in section 79A, which Karnataka Act 56 of 2020 removed.

### Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Coorg?
No, not by purchase. Under the central foreign exchange rules, as summarised by the Ministry of External Affairs, an NRI cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or a farm house in India. An NRI can inherit it.

### Can I build a house on agricultural land I buy?
Only after the land is converted for non-agricultural use under section 95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964.

### Could the old restrictions come back?
They could if the legislature passes a new Act. The government said in 2024 that it intended to. As of 30 October 2026 I have not found such an Act.

### Does the 2020 change apply to irrigated land?
Partly. A-class irrigated land can still be sold only to someone who will use it for agriculture, under section 80 as amended.
