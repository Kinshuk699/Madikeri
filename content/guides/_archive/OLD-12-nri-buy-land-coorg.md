An NRI (a citizen of India living outside India) cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or a farm house in India. An OCI has the same restriction. Since most land sold in Coorg is agricultural or plantation land, this rules out most of it for NRIs, and it is a central foreign-exchange rule, so the 2020 Karnataka land reform does not change it.

I'm selling [land in Galibeedu](https://landincoorg.in/), and this question comes up often enough that it deserves a straight answer with the source quoted.

## What the RBI says

The Reserve Bank's master circular on property, which follows [FEMA Notification 21/2000-RB](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/Notification/PDFs/79MN250915FM.pdf) as amended, states the rule for an NRI in these words:

> "A NRI can acquire by way of purchase any immovable property (other than agricultural land/ plantation property / farm house) in India."

And on transferring land an NRI inherited:

> "He may transfer any immovable property (other than agricultural land or plantation property or farm house) to an Indian Citizen resident outside India or a PIO resident outside India."

The [Ministry of External Affairs summary](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/acquisition-and-transfer-of-immovable-property-in-india.pdf) adds the inheritance point plainly: an NRI who acquires agricultural land, plantation property or a farm house by inheritance may transfer it only to Indian citizens permanently residing in India.

## What an NRI or OCI can buy

| Property | NRI / OCI can buy? |
|---|---|
| Agricultural land | No |
| Plantation property | No |
| Farm house | No |
| Residential or commercial property, or a converted residential plot | Yes, under general permission |
| Agricultural land inherited from a relative | Yes, but may only be sold to a person resident in India |

Payment for what they can buy must come through normal banking channels: inward remittance, or an NRE, FCNR(B) or NRO account. Not traveller's cheques or foreign currency notes.

## What this means for land in Coorg

Coorg land is mostly coffee and agricultural land, so it falls inside the exclusion. A converted residential plot is a different matter, and there the NRI restriction does not bite. If you are buying for an NRI relative, check first whether the land is agricultural or converted, using the [due diligence checklist](/guides/due-diligence-checklist-coorg/), and confirm the classification on the RTC.

This is separate from the Karnataka rule for resident Indians. Since 2020 a resident Indian citizen can buy agricultural land in Karnataka, as explained in [can a non-farmer buy agricultural land in Karnataka](/guides/non-farmer-buy-agricultural-land-karnataka/). The NRI restriction is central law and applies regardless of the state.

## What I could not verify

- Whether a converted residential plot inside a larger formerly-agricultural survey needs anything further before an NRI buys it. That depends on the specific land's records, which only a lawyer can check.

## FAQs

### Can an NRI buy agricultural land in Coorg?
No. The RBI's master circular says an NRI can buy immovable property "other than agricultural land / plantation property / farm house".

### Can an OCI buy land in India?
An OCI has the same restriction as an NRI for agricultural land, plantation property and farm houses.

### Can an NRI inherit land in Coorg?
Yes. Agricultural land or plantation property inherited by an NRI may be held, but it can only be transferred to a person resident in India.

### Can an NRI buy a converted residential plot?
Yes. The exclusion is agricultural land, plantation property and farm houses. Residential and commercial property and converted plots are allowed.

### Does the Karnataka 2020 law change this for NRIs?
No. The Karnataka amendment removed the state's income bar for resident buyers. The NRI restriction is a central foreign-exchange rule.
