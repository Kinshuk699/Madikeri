To buy land in Coorg, first confirm you are allowed to buy it. Then check the land records (RTC, encumbrance certificate and survey sketch), confirm whether the land is converted for what you plan to build, register the sale deed at the Sub-Registrar's office, and have the land records changed into your name. Since 2020, any Indian citizen living in India can buy agricultural land in Karnataka, but NRIs cannot.

We are selling [1.87 acres in Galibeedu](https://landincoorg.in/) ourselves. This page lists the steps in order and links to a separate guide for each one.

## Step 1: Check you are allowed to buy

[Karnataka Act 56 of 2020](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/Acts%20&%20Ordinance/56%20of%202020%20(E).pdf) removed sections 79A and 79B of the Karnataka Land Reforms Act. Those sections had stopped people with more than ₹25 lakh a year of non-agricultural income from buying farmland. A salaried professional can now buy agricultural land. Restrictions remain for A-class irrigated land, land granted to Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes, and NRIs. The full detail, with links to each Act, is in [can a non-farmer buy agricultural land in Karnataka](/guides/non-farmer-buy-agricultural-land-karnataka/).

On 30 October 2026, Google's AI answer to "how to buy land in Coorg" still said non-agriculturists face restrictions. That reflects the law before 2020.

## Step 2: Know what you are buying

Most land sold in Coorg falls into one of three kinds:

- **Agricultural land** that has not been converted. You can farm it. You cannot legally build a house on it until it is converted.
- **Converted land.** The Deputy Commissioner has approved a non-agricultural use, such as residential. Ask for the conversion order and check that its survey number and area match the land you are shown.
- **Plantation land,** usually coffee. The Land Reforms Act treats plantations differently in some respects (section 104). Have your lawyer check how the land is classified. See [coffee estate or dry land](/guides/coffee-estate-vs-dry-land-coorg/).

Ask the seller early which kind it is, and ask for the RTC. If they won't share it, stop there.

## Step 3: Visit, and visit in the rain

Kodagu is a heavy-rain district. The [district administration](https://kodagu.nic.in/en/tourism) puts average annual rainfall at about 310 cm. Roads, drainage and slopes look different between June and September. If you can only visit once, go during the monsoon. Before you visit, read [landslide risk when buying land in Kodagu](/guides/kodagu-landslide-risk-land/).

## Step 4: Check the records

These are the records that matter most. The [due diligence checklist](/guides/due-diligence-checklist-coorg/) covers each one in detail.

| Record | What it tells you | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|
| RTC (pahani) | Owner, survey number, extent, land type, crops, and any liabilities recorded | [Bhoomi](https://landrecords.karnataka.gov.in) |
| Mutation register (MR) | How ownership passed to the current owner | Bhoomi (same portal) |
| Encumbrance certificate (EC) | Registered transactions on the property, such as sales and mortgages | [Kaveri](https://kaveri.karnataka.gov.in) |
| Survey sketch | Boundaries and shape of the survey number | Survey Settlement and Land Records Department |
| Sale deeds (title chain) | Each earlier transfer, usually traced back 30 years | Seller, and certified copies from the Sub-Registrar |

Coorg has land tenures you won't see in most of Karnataka, such as jamma and bane land. I have not yet found an official source I trust that explains how they affect a sale today, so I am not summarising them here. Ask your lawyer to check the tenure shown on the RTC. My notes so far are in [jamma and bane land in Coorg](/guides/jamma-bane-land-coorg/).

## Step 5: Walk the land with the sketch

Make sure the land you are shown is the land on the documents. Walk the boundary with the survey sketch, ideally with a licensed surveyor. Check that the approach road is a recorded right of way, not just a track across someone else's land. Ask whether any boundary touches forest land.

## Step 6: Conversion, if you want to build

To build a house on agricultural land, the land has to be diverted to non-agricultural use under section 95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964. Since [Karnataka Act 02 of 2023](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/Acts%20&%20Ordinance/02%20of%202023%20(E)%20Land%20revenue.pdf), you apply to the Deputy Commissioner with an affidavit:

- If the use you want matches a published Master Plan for that land, the Deputy Commissioner should issue the order within 7 days. If no decision comes within 15 days, approval is deemed granted.
- If there is no published Master Plan, or the land is outside a local planning area, other departments have 15 days to give their opinion. If the Deputy Commissioner does not decide within 30 days, approval is deemed granted.

You still pay the prescribed conversion fee. A conversion obtained with a false affidavit is void, and the land can be forfeited. Since [Act 56 of 2025](https://dpal.karnataka.gov.in/uploads/media_to_upload1759555220.pdf), selling agricultural land for a non-agricultural purpose without conversion carries a ₹1 lakh penalty. Fees and documents are in [converting agricultural land in Kodagu](/guides/convert-agricultural-land-kodagu/).

## Step 7: Agreement

Agree the price and the conditions in writing before any large payment, with your own lawyer checking the draft. A useful condition is that the balance is paid only after the records have been verified.

## Step 8: Stamp duty and registration

The sale deed is registered at the Sub-Registrar's office for the area. Kodagu's district registration office is in Madikeri. Current rates from the [Karnataka Department of Stamps and Registration](https://igr.karnataka.gov.in/page/Stamp+Duty+and+Registration+Fees/en):

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Stamp duty on a conveyance (sale deed) | 5% of the market value, plus surcharge and additional duty |
| Registration fee | 2% of the market value |

The department's page does not state the surcharge and additional duty in percentage terms, so I have not added them up for you. More detail is in [stamp duty and registration for land in Karnataka](/guides/stamp-duty-registration-karnataka-land/).

## Step 9: Mutation

Registration transfers ownership, but the RTC still shows the old owner until it is updated through mutation. After registration, check that the mutation is recorded and that the RTC on Bhoomi shows your name. I have not found an official timeline for this in Kodagu. Follow up with the taluk office until it is done.

## FAQs

### Can a Bangalore professional buy land in Coorg?
Yes, if they are an Indian citizen living in India. The income limit that used to stop salaried buyers was removed by Karnataka Act 56 of 2020.

### Can I build a house on agricultural land in Coorg?
Not until it is converted (diverted) for non-agricultural use under section 95 of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act. You apply to the Deputy Commissioner.

### What are stamp duty and registration charges in Karnataka?
Stamp duty on a sale deed is 5% of the market value plus surcharge and additional duty, and the registration fee is 2% of the market value, according to the Department of Stamps and Registration.

### Where do I check the RTC for land in Kodagu?
On the Bhoomi portal at landrecords.karnataka.gov.in, using the district, taluk, village and survey number.

### Can an NRI buy land in Coorg?
An NRI cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or a farm house in India. An NRI can buy other immovable property, such as a converted residential plot.
